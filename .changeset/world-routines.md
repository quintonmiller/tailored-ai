The town has people in it while agents have nothing to do. Each agent has a day of its own, drawn from its name and the date, shaped by what it is for and by the hours it keeps when it runs on its own: up in the morning, home for breakfast, then stretches out (a coffee at the new café on the square, a bench on the square or by the pond, reading in a shared place, sitting in the meeting room of a room it belongs to while that room is lively, dropping in on an agent it often messages or shares rooms with) between stretches at home, and to bed at night, its windows dark. Two or more at one table, bench or home keep each other company: they face each other and talk with their hands, nod and laugh under a small chatting sign, with no words made up. Work always wins: a body is never moved while its agent is busy, a turn starting sends it home at once (walking off the square as you watch), a talk's visit is never written over, and a paused agent stays home asleep; but one you walk up to and talk to face to face stays where it is, with you. No model is asked: the places plugin looks once a minute and writes the same memory-backed `visit` records a talk does, with `errand`, `spot`, `with` and `what`, so every page sees the same bodies in the same seats. `routines: quiet | normal | busy | off` in the places plugin's config says how busy the town is; normal is the default. Meeting rooms show their conversation: a whiteboard by the task board with the room's last four lines' first words and who said them, a card on the door and the map saying when the room last talked, from a new people-only `chatter` record per room. Buildings other than homes light up while someone is in them and go dark at night once empty; a home whose owner is out is dark after dark. New kinds of thing: `cafe_table`, `cafe_chair`, `coffee_cart` and `whiteboard`, and every bench in town seats two. The `room.message` event now also carries the transport's own name for whoever spoke (`authorLabel`) and how many files came with it (`files`), so a plugin can name a speaker no identity was resolved for as the room's transcript does.
