The 3D world reads in a crowd. Round a busy meeting table, tags step aside
up or across instead of piling up, a tag that had to move has a thin line
down to its head, and in a crowd everyone's but the speaker's and the one you
point at is drawn faint, with any that would stack a column high put away.
Whoever is talking keeps its tag over its own head, ahead of the one you
stand by, and its bubble's tail runs right to the edge of that tag, leaning
round anyone standing in line rather than at the body behind. A bubble pulled
down under the buttons goes beside its speaker's face, or just under its
tag, never over its own speaker's tag or another speaker's, and in a busy
room the newest line wins over an earlier one in its way. Pointing at any
face you can use, a model on a wall too, lights it and says "Use … · click
or F", the same thing a click does, and a hidden tag or bubble never takes
the pointer. Rearranging a room, a table or a café table brings its chairs
and a rug what stands on it (Alt moves one piece alone, and keeps it alone
when you turn it), nothing you add or copy lands where someone stands, and a
group too big to move at once is refused in words: the `places` edit route
takes `with` on a move and `avoid` on an add. The trail of where you are
keeps to one short line, folds its middle when it must, opens out under the
pointer without folding up again on the way to a step, and fades while what
you look at is behind it; the map wraps names and what a busy agent is doing
before cutting them, and marks every link that ends a visit; gates at a
room's open side are a gateway that all but goes when someone stands behind
it, with its name kept on the mat at its foot and the pointer going through
the rest; and the wheel, or a pinch on a phone, flies you while you fly.
